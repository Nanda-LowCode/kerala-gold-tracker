## The Price You See on Google Is Not What You Pay

If you have ever Googled "gold rate today" and then walked into a jewellery shop in Kochi, Thrissur, or Trivandrum, you already know the number on your screen and the number on the counter are different. Sometimes by hundreds of rupees per gram. That gap confuses first-time buyers and even experienced gold investors.

The reason is simple: Google shows the **international spot price**, while Kerala jewellers follow the **AKGSMA Board Rate**. These are two completely different numbers, calculated in different ways, for different purposes.

## What Is the International Gold Spot Price?

The spot price — also called the LBMA price or COMEX price — is the wholesale rate for **pure 24-karat gold** on international commodity markets. It is quoted in US dollars per troy ounce (31.1 grams). Major trading centres in London, New York, and Shanghai set this price through continuous electronic trading.

When Google or a financial app shows you the "gold rate," it is usually converting this USD-per-ounce number into INR-per-gram using the current exchange rate. This gives you a rough idea of where the market sits globally, but it is **not a retail price**. No jeweller in Kerala — or anywhere in India — sells gold at the spot price.

## What Is the AKGSMA Board Rate?

The **All Kerala Gold and Silver Merchants Association (AKGSMA)** is the trade body that sets the daily gold rate used by almost every jeweller in Kerala. Every morning, the AKGSMA committee considers several factors and publishes the official board rate for 22-karat and 24-karat gold per gram.

This board rate is what Malabar Gold, Kalyan Jewellers, Bhima, Joy Alukkas, and virtually every other shop in the state uses as their base price. It is the rate you actually pay when you buy a gold chain, ring, or bangle — before making charges.

### How the Board Rate Is Set Each Morning

The AKGSMA committee meets early each morning and looks at three things:

1. **The overnight movement in international gold** — London and New York trade while India sleeps, so the global price may have risen or fallen.
2. **The USD-INR exchange rate** — since gold is imported in dollars, the rupee's strength directly affects the landed cost.
3. **The previous day's closing rate on MCX** — India's Multi Commodity Exchange gives a domestic benchmark that already factors in import duty and GST.

Based on these inputs, the committee publishes the day's 22K and 24K rates, typically by 10:00–10:15 AM IST. If the global market moves sharply during the day, they may revise the rate — but this is uncommon.

## Why Are They Different? The Five-Layer Gap

The Kerala board rate is higher than the converted spot price because of real costs that get added at each step:

1. **Import Duty** — India imports nearly all its gold. The central government levies a customs duty (currently around 6%) plus an Agriculture Infrastructure Cess (2.5%). This single tax adds thousands of rupees per 10 grams.

2. **GST** — A 3% Goods and Services Tax is applied on the landed cost of gold, further raising the base price.

3. **USD to INR Conversion** — The spot price is in dollars. A weaker rupee means higher gold prices in India, even if the global price stays flat.

4. **Refining and Logistics** — International gold arrives as large bars. It must be refined, assayed, transported, insured, and stored before reaching retail. Each step adds cost.

5. **Local Market Premium** — India is the world's second-largest gold consumer. Strong domestic demand — especially during wedding and festival seasons like Onam and Akshaya Tritiya — can push the Kerala rate above what the math alone would suggest.

### A Worked Example: Spot to Board Rate

Here is a simplified example showing how a global gold price of $2,400/oz becomes the rate you see at a Kerala jeweller (numbers are illustrative):

| Step | Calculation | Price |
|------|------------|-------|
| International spot price | $2,400 per troy ounce | $2,400/oz |
| Convert to grams | $2,400 ÷ 31.1 grams | $77.17/gram |
| Convert to INR (₹84/$) | $77.17 × 84 | ₹6,482/gram (24K) |
| Add import duty (6% + 2.5% cess) | ₹6,482 × 1.085 | ₹7,033/gram |
| Add GST (3%) | ₹7,033 × 1.03 | ₹7,244/gram |
| Refining, logistics, premium | ~₹50–150/gram | ₹7,350/gram (24K) |
| Convert to 22K (×0.916) | ₹7,350 × 0.916 | **₹6,733/gram (22K)** |

The actual board rate on this day might be ₹6,700–6,800/gram, depending on market premium and committee discretion. The key point: between the spot price and what you pay, taxes and duties alone add roughly **12–13%**.

## 22K vs 24K — Why Kerala Mostly Trades in 22K

The spot price refers to **24-karat (99.9% pure) gold**. But most jewellery sold in Kerala is **22 karat (91.6% pure)**, because pure 24K gold is too soft for everyday ornaments. The board rate publishes both, but the 22K rate is the one that matters for most buyers.

To estimate 22K from 24K: multiply the 24K price by 0.916. The actual board rate may differ slightly because of rounding and association conventions.

If you are comparing rates, make sure you are comparing the same karat. A "cheaper" rate might just be a 22K number being compared to a 24K number.

## What About Making Charges?

The board rate is the base price of the gold itself. On top of this, jewellers add **making charges** (also called wastage or manufacturing charges) — typically 8–25% depending on the ornament's complexity. A plain bangle might carry 8–10% making charges, while an intricate temple necklace could be 20% or more.

This means the final price you pay at the counter is:

**Final price = Board rate × weight + Making charges + 3% GST on making charges**

## When Does the Gap Widen or Narrow?

The difference between the international price and the Kerala board rate is not constant. It changes based on:

- **Rupee depreciation** — when the rupee weakens against the dollar, Indian gold prices rise even if global prices are flat. In 2024–2025, rupee weakness added ₹200–400/gram to the India premium.
- **Duty changes** — the government has revised gold import duty multiple times. The July 2024 cut from 15% to 6% narrowed the gap significantly.
- **Festival/wedding demand** — during peak buying seasons (Onam, Diwali, Akshaya Tritiya, wedding months), strong demand can push the board rate above what pure cost calculations would suggest.
- **Global volatility** — when gold spikes or crashes overnight, the board rate may lag by a day as the committee waits for the market to settle.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is the gold rate in Kerala different from Chennai or Mumbai?

Each state or region has its own jewellers' association that sets rates independently. The AKGSMA sets Kerala's rate; Tamil Nadu has its own association, and Mumbai jewellers follow yet another. The differences are usually small (₹10–50/gram) and reflect local demand, logistics costs, and association conventions.

### Is the AKGSMA rate legally binding?

No. It is a recommended rate, not a government-mandated price. However, virtually all major jewellers in Kerala follow it because deviating would confuse customers and invite scrutiny. You can think of it as an industry standard.

### Can I buy gold at the international spot price in India?

Not directly. Even digital gold platforms (like those offered by banks and fintech apps) price gold at rates that include import duty and GST. The spot price is a wholesale benchmark — retail buyers in India always pay more. Our [digital gold guide](/blog/digital-gold-india-guide) explains the options.

### Why does the board rate sometimes not change when the global price moves?

The AKGSMA committee considers multiple factors. A small drop in the global price might be offset by a weaker rupee, resulting in no change. Or the committee may wait for the MCX session to confirm the trend before adjusting. Rates are typically revised only when the net change is significant.

### How much gold can NRIs bring when flying home?

NRIs returning to India have a duty-free gold allowance under CBIC Baggage Rules. The amount depends on gender and time spent abroad. See our [complete NRI gold import guide](/blog/nri-gold-rules-india-2026) for the latest 2026 limits and duty rates.
