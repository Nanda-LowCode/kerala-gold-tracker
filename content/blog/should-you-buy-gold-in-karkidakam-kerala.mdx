Ask an older Malayali whether you should buy gold in **Karkidakam**, and the answer is usually
a firm no. It's the month families traditionally hold off on big purchases, weddings and new
beginnings. But does that tradition actually cost you anything — and is the gold rate any
different? Here's the honest picture for 2026.

## When Is Karkidakam 2026?

Karkidakam is the **last month of the Malayalam calendar**, running roughly from
**17 July to 16 August 2026**, just before Chingam 1 (the Malayalam New Year) and the Onam
season. It's the peak of the monsoon in Kerala.

## Why Karkidakam Is Considered a "Bad" Month to Buy Gold

The caution around Karkidakam is cultural and historical, not financial:

- **The lean month (Panja Masam).** Historically Karkidakam was the hardest month of the
  agrarian year — heavy monsoon, no harvest, and tight household budgets. The phrase
  *"Karkidaka kashtam"* (the hardship of Karkidakam) comes from this. Big spending simply
  wasn't done.
- **Ramayana Masam.** Karkidakam is the spiritual *Ramayana month* — households read the
  Adhyatma Ramayana, focus on health (Karkidaka Ayurveda treatments, *karkidaka kanji*) and
  devotion rather than shopping or celebration.
- **Inauspicious for new things.** Weddings, housewarmings, and major purchases are
  traditionally avoided and saved for the auspicious months that follow — Chingam, Onam and
  Vishu.

So the "don't buy gold in Karkidakam" rule is really about **timing of auspicious purchases**,
not about gold being a worse deal.

## Does the Gold Rate Change in Karkidakam?

**No.** The [Kerala board rate](/) set by the AKGSMA tracks international gold prices and the
rupee — it has nothing to do with the Malayalam calendar. Gold doesn't get cheaper or more
expensive *because* it's Karkidakam. You can watch the
[30-day trend](/gold-rate-history) and check

What *does* change is **demand** — and that's where the contrarian opportunity is.

## The Contrarian Case: Why Karkidakam Can Be a Smart Time to Buy

Because tradition keeps most buyers away, Karkidakam is the **quietest retail month** of the
year for Kerala jewellers. For a purely financial buyer — an investor, or someone stocking up
ahead of a later occasion — that quiet works in your favour:

- **More room to negotiate making charges.** Showrooms are empty and staff have time. Making
  charges (8–25% of the gold value) are the one negotiable number, and you have more leverage
  when shops are chasing slow-month sales. See how much that matters in our
  [making charges explained](/blog/gold-making-charges-explained-kerala) guide.
- **You skip the Onam rush and premium.** Demand — and the firmness on making charges — climbs
  sharply once Chingam and [Onam](/blog/onam-gold-buying-guide-kerala-2026) arrive. Buying just
  *before* the season can mean a calmer, better-negotiated purchase.
- **No price penalty.** Since the board rate is identical in Karkidakam, you lose nothing on
  the metal by buying now versus during the festival.

## So — Should You Buy or Wait?

It comes down to *why* you're buying:

| Your situation | Recommendation |
|----------------|----------------|
| **Investment / coins / bars** | Karkidakam is fine — even smart. No rate penalty, more negotiating room. |
| **Stocking up before Onam or a wedding** | Buying in the quiet month can beat the festival rush on making charges. |
| **An auspicious or ceremonial purchase** (wedding gold, gift, first buy) | If you or your family observe the tradition, wait for **Chingam 1, Onam or Vishu**. The peace of mind matters more than a small making-charge saving. |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it good to buy gold in Karkidakam?**
Financially, yes — the Kerala board rate is the same in Karkidakam as any other month, and the
low festive demand gives you more room to negotiate making charges. Traditionally, many
Keralites avoid buying gold in Karkidakam and wait for the auspicious months (Chingam, Onam,
Vishu), especially for ceremonial purchases.

**Why do people avoid buying gold in Karkidakam?**
Because Karkidakam was historically the lean monsoon "hardship" month and is the spiritual
Ramayana month — a time for health and devotion, not celebration or big purchases. The custom
is cultural, not about the gold price.

**Does gold cost less in Karkidakam?**
No. The board rate follows global gold prices and the rupee, not the Malayalam calendar. Any
saving comes from negotiating making charges in a quiet sales month, not from a lower rate.

**When does Karkidakam end in 2026?**
Karkidakam 2026 runs roughly from 17 July to 16 August, ending just before Chingam 1 and the
Onam season.
